# Made in India. Priced for India? — Evidence Upgrade Findings

## What changed
This version keeps the original benchmark (iPhone 17 256GB) and project question intact, but upgrades three analytical layers that were already approved:

1. **Consumer affordability:** household final consumption expenditure per capita at PPP is now the primary consumer-burden proxy.
2. **Tax normalization:** standard VAT/GST is removed directionally where the tax treatment is defensible.
3. **Historical context:** India iPhone production growth is compared with base-model India pricing over time.

The older GDP-per-capita PPP analysis is retained as a secondary robustness check.

## Strongest findings

### 1. India is not the most expensive market in absolute dollars
In the 13-market nominal-price sample, India is mid-pack rather than #1 or #2. This means the strongest story is **not** “India has the world's highest iPhone price.”

### 2. India looks much more extreme when affordability is measured
Using household-consumption PPP per capita as a consumer-spending proxy:

- India: iPhone price ≈ **14.9%** of annual household-consumption PPP, or **1.79 months**
- China: ≈ **9.4%**, or **1.13 months**
- Singapore: ≈ **3.1%**, or **0.37 months**
- United States: ≈ **1.6%**, or **0.19 months**

Within this core comparable sample, India has the highest burden.

**Interpretation:** the key issue is not simply sticker price; it is sticker price relative to consumer purchasing power.

### 3. Tax normalization changes the India–US comparison materially
Current India listed price: **₹99,900**, about **US$1,047** using the workbook FX rate.

Removing 18% GST mathematically:

`₹99,900 / 1.18 ≈ ₹84,661`

At the same FX rate this is about **US$887**, below the **US$929** Apple US unlocked list price before state/local sales tax.

This is only a **tax bridge**, not a full cost decomposition. It does not prove GST is the sole reason for the observed retail-price gap.

### 4. Manufacturing scale does not mechanically translate into lower list prices
Reported India iPhone production value:

- FY23: **>$7B**
- FY24: **~$14B**
- FY25: **~$22B**
- FY26: **~$26B**

That is roughly a **3.7×** increase from the conservative FY23 value to FY26.

During this period, base-model India launch prices stayed around **₹79,900** through iPhone 16. The iPhone 17 launched at **₹82,900**, but its base storage increased from 128GB to 256GB, so this is not pure like-for-like inflation. The same iPhone 17 256GB later moved to **₹99,900**, which is directly comparable to its own launch price.

### 5. The defensible project thesis is now clearer
The evidence supports:

> **“Made in India” describes where important manufacturing and assembly activity happens. It does not mean the entire value chain, tax structure, or retail-pricing decision has been localized.**

The project should therefore explain **why local manufacturing and local retail price can move differently**, rather than assuming manufacturing must directly reduce MRP.

## Portfolio-ready analytical story

1. **The paradox:** India has become a major iPhone production hub.
2. **The correction:** India is not necessarily among the two highest nominal-price markets.
3. **The reveal:** affordability looks far more challenging than nominal price.
4. **The decomposition:** taxes materially alter cross-country list-price comparisons.
5. **The manufacturing layer:** production scale grew rapidly, but local value addition and imported inputs still matter.
6. **The conclusion:** sticker price, affordability, manufacturing scale, and localization are different metrics and should not be conflated.

## Data-integrity rules
- **REPORTED:** directly published by Apple, government, regulator, or credible reporting.
- **CALCULATED:** arithmetic derived from reported inputs.
- **ESTIMATED:** external estimate clearly attributed.
- **MODELED:** scenario assumption created for analysis.

Do not describe tax-normalized price as Apple's pre-tax “cost” or “margin.”
Do not claim causal pricing intent without evidence.
Do not compare iPhone 17 256GB launch price with 128GB predecessors as pure price inflation.

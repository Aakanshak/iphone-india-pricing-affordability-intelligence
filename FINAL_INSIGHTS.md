# Made in India. Priced for India?
## Final Portfolio Insight Selection

### Core question
India has become a major iPhone manufacturing hub. Why can an iPhone still feel expensive to an Indian consumer, and is sticker price even the right metric?

### Final thesis
**“Made in India” does not automatically mean “priced cheaply for India.” Manufacturing scale, local value addition, taxes, FX, consumer purchasing power, and resale economics are different layers.**

## The 6 insights that survive into the final case study

### 1. India is not the world's most expensive iPhone market
In the selected 13-market iPhone 17 256GB snapshot, India ranks 7th by USD-equivalent listed price.

**Why this matters:** it corrects the provocative starting assumption instead of forcing the data to fit it.

### 2. Affordability is the stronger India story
The consumer-spending PPP proxy makes the contrast much sharper. In the comparable India/China/Singapore/US core sample, the iPhone represents roughly:
- India: 14.9% of annual consumption PPP, ~1.79 months
- China: 9.4%, ~1.13 months
- Singapore: 3.1%, ~0.37 months
- US: 1.6%, ~0.19 months

**Decision insight:** compare price to purchasing power, not only currency-converted MRP.

### 3. Taxes materially change the India–US price comparison
India listed price ≈ $1,047 versus $929 US unlocked list price before US state/local sales tax.

Removing India's 18% GST mathematically gives ≈ ₹84,661 / $887.

**Important:** this is a tax bridge, not Apple's cost or margin. It shows why tax treatment must be normalized before making cross-country pricing claims.

### 4. Manufacturing scale and retail price are not the same metric
Reported India iPhone production rose from >$7B in FY23 to ~$26B in FY26.

At the same time, base-model pricing followed its own path. iPhone 17 also changed base storage to 256GB, so historical comparisons require a comparability flag.

**Decision insight:** local assembly can scale without retail MRP falling proportionally.

### 5. “Made in India” is not the same as “fully localized”
FY26 Apple India value addition is estimated/reported around 12–17%, averaging ~14.5%, while a large component/other import bill remains.

**Decision insight:** assembly location does not remove imported-input exposure, FX effects, duties, taxes, or pricing strategy.

### 6. Sticker price is not total ownership cost
At a modeled 3-year hold and 40% resale:
- iPhone 17: ~₹1,665/month economic ownership cost
- Galaxy S26: ~₹1,467/month under identical assumptions

Galaxy starts ₹11,901 cheaper, but if Galaxy retains 40%, the iPhone needs roughly 47.1% resale retention for modeled TCO to converge.

**Decision insight:** purchase price alone does not determine which premium phone is cheaper to own.

---

## What NOT to put in the headline
Do not claim:
- India has the world's second-highest iPhone price.
- Making iPhones in India should automatically make them cheap.
- GST alone explains Apple's India pricing.
- The tax-normalized remainder is Apple's margin.
- The iPhone definitely has higher resale than Samsung without observed resale data.

## Recommended portfolio headline
# Made in India. Priced for India?
### Why local iPhone manufacturing doesn't automatically mean cheaper iPhones.

## Recommended recruiter-facing one-liner
